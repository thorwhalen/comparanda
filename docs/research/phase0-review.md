# Phase 0 review

An adversarial review of the round-1 research *recommendations* — not their citations, which
were audited separately when the research landed. Two rounds were run; the second exists because
the first was methodologically broken, and that is the most useful thing in this file.

## The calibration lesson, first

Round 1 instructed its skeptics to **default to refuted under uncertainty**. It refuted 21 of 21
candidates it managed to examine — a result that carries no information, because a sufficiently
skeptical reviewer refutes everything. Round 2 replaced that with a **fair adjudicator** required
to open every named file, read the full surrounding context, verify quotes character-for-character,
and return CONFIRMED / PARTLY / REFUTED with the decisive quote. Anything not refuted then got an
independent second opinion.

The two rounds disagree sharply, and round 2 is the one to trust.

| | round 1 (refutation-biased) | round 2 (fair adjudication) |
|---|---|---|
| candidates examined | 21 | 56 |
| confirmed | 0 | 7 |
| partly real | 0 | 22 |
| refuted | 21 | 27 |

**Round 2's severity finding is the sharpest result here.** Twelve candidates claimed `blocking`.
After adjudication, **not one remained blocking** — every single one was downgraded. The review
lenses were systematically right that *something* was there and systematically wrong about how
much it mattered. Read every severity claim in this file as an upper bound.

A note on why this matters beyond bookkeeping: these repositories exist to make the difference
between an asserted judgement and a checked one visible. Applying that to their own review is not
ceremony. Round 1's output looked like a clean bill of health and was worth nothing.

## How to use this

Most surviving entries are **notes to attach to a pending ADR decision**, not standalone work.
When you settle an item in `docs/adr/PENDING-ACTIONS.md`, scan here for entries naming it. The
seven CONFIRMED entries became issues; the PARTLY entries did not, because their fix is usually
an edit to a draft you are about to settle anyway.

Delete an entry once it is resolved either way.

<details><summary>`preference` is declared per (criterion, measure) in comparanda and requested per criterion by rubricator — and nothing says which measure dominance reads</summary>

- **why refuted:** Every quote in the candidate is accurate, but each one is cut immediately before the clause that answers it, and the three asserted breakages fail — two on the documents, one on its own internal logic.

1. The granularity is not unsettled, and it is not new. `comparanda: docs/adr/0003-domain-model-and-terminology.md` is **accepted**, and its point 3 already fixes the pattern: level of measurement is "declared per `(criterion, measure)` with a per-criterion default." Draft ADR-0018 opens "Each `(criterion, measure)` declares, **beside its level of measurement**" — it is deliberately hanging `preference` off the site ADR-0003 already established, and the source research says so in the sentence the candidate truncates: `comparanda: docs/research/sections/c1-terminology.md` §"Schema — the one blocking change" reads "Add a direction of preference beside the level of measurement, per `(criterion, measure)`, **with the same per-criterion default**". So there is no criterion-vs-pair question to settle: it is the pair, declared at the pair, defaulted from the criterion, exactly as an accepted ADR already does for `level`.

2. Breakage (a) is refuted by that default plus the field default. rubricator does not "have to emit a `preference` and a `range` for `confidence` on every criterion"; it emits the criterion's declaration once and the pair inherits — which is precisely what the per-criterion default exists for ("to keep the common case terse", domain-model.md Correction 3). ADR-0018's own Consequences confirm the authoring granularity in words: "**Authoring a criterion costs two more fields**" — two per criterion, not two per (criterion, measure). The ADR's title is likewise criterion-level: "Criteria carry a direction of preference, a declared range, and optional thresholds."

3. Breakage (b) is refuted twice over. First, the merged field design in `comparanda: docs/research/findings-terminology.md` §2 declares `preference: Preference; // default { kind: 'none' }` — `none` is not merely "the only safe value with nothing saying so", it is the shipped default, in both the c1 sketch and the merged one. Second, and more fundamentally, `confidence: increasing` cannot make evidence quality "a legal dominance dimension", because dominance is never computed across measures. The core API in c1-terminology.md takes the measure as an explicit parameter — `findDominated(matrix, *, measure: MeasureId, tolerance?)`, and likewise `encodeRelativeToDatum(..., measure: MeasureId, ...)` and `findNonDiscriminatingCriteria(..., measure: MeasureId, ...)`. Dominance runs over criteria *for one chosen measure*; adding a preference to the `confidence` slot adds no axis to a score-dominance computation. No alternative can be "declared dominated for being less well-evidenced" by any operation these documents define.

4. Breakage (c) is self-inconsistent with (b) and its consequence does not follow. (b) needs dominance to quantify over (criterion, measure) pairs; (c) correctly observes it quantifies over criteria. Both cannot be true. And "the dominance implementation has to pick a measure and neither ADR names one" inverts the design: the *caller* picks, via `measure: MeasureId`, and ADR-0019 requires the basis be "named in every result". A drafted ADR summarising a research section is not obliged to restate the function signature that section specifies.

5. The closing claim — "the two repos both believe request 6 is a confirmation of a settled field; they are confirming different fields" — is refuted by the register itself. `comparanda: docs/cross-repo-coordination.md` §7 files request 6 with the disposition column blank and states "the disposition column is filled in by `comparanda`, in the mirrored issues, and nowhere else", then notes requests "1, 2, 3 and 6 are criterion-shaped … field additions **once the shape is agreed**". The shape being agreed at Gate 2a is the documented mechanism, not a contradiction discovered in it.
